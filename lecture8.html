But SGD is not always easy to optimize

Four challenges

  1. Choosing the learning rate
  2. Ill-conditioning and saddle regions
  3. Different gradient scales across coordinates
  4. Noisy or misleading update directions

Challenge 1: the learning rate

Why change the learning rate over time?

Large steps early to move fast, small steps late to settle. Schedules help, but must still be chosen and tuned.

Challenge 2: a narrow valley

One learning rate, very different curvatures: it bounces across the steep direction and crawls along the shallow one.

Challenge 3: tiny gradients near a saddle

Gradient descent slides toward the saddle, stalls where the gradient is tiny, and only slowly escapes. Not every critical point is a minimum.

Which optimizer should we use?

  • Start with Adam or AdamW for many deep-learning tasks.
  • Compare momentum SGD for image-classification tasks.
  • Consider AdaGrad for sparse-feature applications.
  • Tune the learning rate and evaluate validation performance.

No method always has the fastest convergence or the best generalization.

Curriculum learning: what we have learned

Who decides what is easy?
A human rule (short sentences first), or the model’s own loss: self-paced learning.

When does it help?
Mostly when training time is short or the labels are noisy.

When does it not?
With a full training budget, random shuffling is usually just as good.

Where is it essential?
Reinforcement learning: agents are given harder tasks as they improve.
